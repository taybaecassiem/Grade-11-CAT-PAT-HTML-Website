QUESTIONNAIRE RESULTS

A questionnaire was conducted to determine what people in my local area and school (Abbotts HS Centurion) did with their unused electronic devices and what their opinions on e-waste are.

  • The most popular method of handling unused electronic devices was to leave it at home - 95% of student respondents.
  • Not many students recycle, or have ever recycled, unused electronic devices.
  • Many people do not throw electrical equipment and devices in the bin – 26% do.
  • People must not know that devices can be returned to the manufacturer as many do not return devices to manufacturers – only 26% do.

Only 22% of respondents said they have more than ten unused devices at their homes.


Most people (41%) were unsure if they were aware of the environmental dangers related to e-waste. Most respondents (33%) claimed that they cared very much about the environmental dangers of e-waste. On average, respondents cared slightly. 7% of respondents admitted that they do not care about the negative effect e-waste has on the environment.

WHAT HAPPENS TO E-WASTE AT LANDFILLS?
  • E-waste contaminates the soil. The toxic substances found in components of electric devices seep into the soil.
  • When it rains, the rainfall will wash these same harmful chemicals into water bodies and help it to travel further than the area of the landfill. This will cause water pollution.
  • Some landfills get rid of all the waste by burning. This burning of waste releases toxic gases into the atmosphere which cause air pollution.
  • Some devices like batteries leak hazardous material. Batteries can leak chemicals, such as lithium, and even cause fires if they burst.

E-WASTE BURNING

Waste is burned mainly because municipalities fail to properly collect and dispose of e-waste. The burning of all kinds of wastes (including e-waste) releases harmful chemicals which cause pollution and health issues.


E-waste contains many valuable resources which can be reused if they are recycled properly. When e-waste is not recycled properly, it can release toxic chemicals which can cause harm to human health and the environment. Toxins found in e-waste are released when they are not properly disposed.

Many harmful activities take place which are harmful to the environment, including:

  • scavenging,
  • dumping on land or water bodies,
  • landfilling with normal waste,
  • burning or heating,
  • acid baths or acid leaching,
  • stripping and shedding plastic coatings,
  • and manually disassembling equipment.
